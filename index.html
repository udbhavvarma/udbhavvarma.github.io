What the work taught me.

Six principles I return to when an idea meets reality.

When the room needs a shared picture.Clarity is a competitive advantage.+

Vague briefs create expensive certainty. I surface assumptions and unknowns early, so the team can disagree with the same problem in view.

When the first version meets reality.Ship, then learn.+

A useful version in someone’s hands teaches more than a polished idea waiting for permission. I ship to replace opinions with evidence.

When every request sounds urgent.The metric is the strategy.+

I ask what should change for the user and the business. That answer makes priorities clearer and gives the next decision something concrete to improve.

When the solution meets its constraints.Engineering is a partner, not a vendor.+

The best product decisions happen with engineering in the room early. Reliability, constraints, and technical debt belong in the product conversation.

When a model enters the product loop.AI is infrastructure now.+

I treat model behavior as product behavior: something to evaluate, trace, observe, and improve—not a feature label added at the end.

When a good product still needs an audience.Distribution beats product.+

How people discover, adopt, and return to a product changes what should be built. Growth and distribution are part of the experience from the beginning.
