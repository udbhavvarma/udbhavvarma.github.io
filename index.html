// principles.md

On a serious note.

A few things I genuinely believe about building products. No irony here.

01
Clarity is a competitive advantage
The best product decisions come from being brutally honest about what you know and what you don't. Vague specs are expensive. Clear ones are underrated.
02
Ship, then learn
Perfect is the enemy of useful. I'd rather have a working MVP in the hands of users than a polished prototype in a Figma file. Feedback is data. Ship to get it.
03
The metric is the strategy
If you can't tie a decision back to a number that matters, you're guessing. I define north star metrics before writing a single line of a PRD.
04
Engineering is a partner, not a vendor
The best products I've shipped happened when engineering was in the room from day one. Constraints are features. Technical debt is a product risk.
05
AI is infrastructure now
We're past the "AI features" phase. The question is how deeply and responsibly it's embedded into the core product loop — not whether it should be.
06
Distribution beats product
A great product no one finds is a prototype. Growth, GTM, and performance marketing aren't afterthoughts — they're part of the product. I take them seriously.
